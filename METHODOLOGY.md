# Methodology and evidence classification

**Phase 1: Content preparation (before scheduling)**
1. Review the development experience available at the time.
2. Identify transferable lessons.
3. Group them into the five workstreams.
4. Draft the 15 posts and graphics with ChatGPT's assistance.
5. Review claims, assumptions and disclosure boundaries.
6. Owner approves.
7. Owner schedules the posts manually.

**Phase 2: Portfolio assurance (after scheduling)**
- **Stage A, evidence matrix:** each post was mapped to milestones and evidence, and its limitations were recorded. Sources were the milestone register, QA report, production acceptance report and owner-supplied screenshots.
- **Stage B, visual review:** each graphic was checked against the evidence, its disclosure labels, privacy and legibility. Where a graphic could be over-read, the case-study text carries a qualification. The graphic itself is not altered.
- **Stage C, documentation:** public-safe GitHub and Behance materials were drafted from the two stages above.

**Evidence rules**
- Each post module records its evidence source and verification / timing at the module level, using the evidence-classification reference below. Supporting milestones, illustrative or retrospective elements, and limitations and disclosures are recorded in its case-study sections. This does not mean that every individual claim has a separate inline source and timing label.
- A Lovable-reported test means the project records say the test ran. It is not an independent audit.
- Owner-verified means the owner observed the result and supplied evidence, such as a screenshot. Owner-confirmed means the owner's statement only.
- Retrospective portfolio framings are labelled Inferred. They are never presented as artefacts that existed during development.

**Public-safety rule.** Milestone IDs may be shown. Customer references, payment identifiers, account emails, admin screens, credentials and security mechanisms are never shown.

## Evidence-classification reference

The **source** dimension describes where a claim comes from: Documented, Lovable-reported, Owner-verified, Owner-confirmed, Inferred, Illustrative, or Unverified.

The **timing and verification** dimension describes how it was established: Report-recorded, Inspected during review, Performed at the time, or Not applicable for conceptual scenarios. A reported test is not an independent audit. For historically performed tests, the current portfolio review did not rerun them.
